# Chapter 16 — Future Terms

> **Technology, education, and industry continuously evolve. This chapter establishes how new terminology is introduced while preserving the stability and consistency of the Simplicion Academy vocabulary.**

---

# 16.1 Purpose

This chapter defines the governance framework for introducing future terminology into Simplicion Academy.

It ensures that new educational, technical, operational, and AI-related concepts can be incorporated without creating inconsistency or ambiguity.

Future terms shall be introduced in a structured and controlled manner.

---

# 16.2 Scope

This chapter applies to all future terminology, including but not limited to:

- Educational terminology
- Technical terminology
- Artificial Intelligence terminology
- Platform terminology
- Career terminology
- Assessment terminology
- Documentation terminology
- Governance terminology

---

# 16.3 Guiding Principles

Future terminology must be:

- Necessary
- Clearly Defined
- Non-Conflicting
- Consistent
- Search Friendly
- Human Readable
- Machine Readable
- Globally Applicable

---

# 16.4 Eligibility Criteria

A new term may be introduced only if it satisfies one or more of the following conditions:

- Represents a new educational concept.
- Represents new technology.
- Represents a new platform capability.
- Represents an industry-recognized concept.
- Improves clarity.
- Eliminates ambiguity.
- Supports future platform scalability.

---

# 16.5 New Term Proposal

Every proposed term MUST include the following information.

| Field | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Proposed Term | Official name being proposed |
| Category | Educational, Technical, AI, Governance, etc. |
| Definition | Canonical definition |
| Purpose | Why the term is required |
| Usage Examples | Practical examples |
| Related Terms | Existing terminology relationships |
| Proposed Abbreviation | If applicable |
| Status | Proposed |

---

# 16.6 Review Process

Every proposed term follows the same governance workflow.

```text
Proposal
    │
    ▼
Initial Review
    │
    ▼
Technical Validation
    │
    ▼
Terminology Review
    │
    ▼
Approval
    │
    ▼
Publication
    │
    ▼
Official Vocabulary
```

No proposed term becomes official until the approval stage has been completed.

---

# 16.7 Categories

Future terms should be classified into one of the following categories.

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Educational | Learning and curriculum terminology |
| Assessment | Evaluation terminology |
| Career | Professional development terminology |
| Technical | Software and engineering terminology |
| Artificial Intelligence | AI concepts and technologies |
| Platform | Product and system terminology |
| Governance | Standards and compliance terminology |
| Analytics | Reporting and measurement terminology |
| Security | Privacy and security terminology |
| Operations | Administrative terminology |

---

# 16.8 Naming Rules

New terminology MUST:

- Follow Chapter 13 (Naming Standards).
- Follow Chapter 14 (Controlled Vocabulary).
- Follow Chapter 15 (Abbreviations), where applicable.
- Avoid duplicate meanings.
- Avoid conflicting terminology.
- Use professional language.

---

# 16.9 Versioning

New terminology shall be introduced through Foundation Standards version updates.

Examples:

| Change | Version Impact |
|---------|----------------|
| Typographical correction | Patch |
| New approved term | Minor |
| Breaking terminology change | Major |

Version history shall record every terminology addition, modification, or retirement.

---

# 16.10 Deprecation

A term may be deprecated when:

- It is obsolete.
- It has been replaced.
- It causes ambiguity.
- It no longer aligns with Academy architecture.
- Industry standards have significantly changed.

Deprecated terms remain documented for historical reference but MUST NOT be used in new documentation.

---

# 16.11 Legacy Terms

Legacy terminology may continue to exist within archived documentation.

Legacy terms should:

- Be clearly identified.
- Reference the preferred replacement.
- Not appear in new standards.
- Be migrated when practical.

---

# 16.12 Reserved Future Terms

The Academy may reserve terminology for planned initiatives.

Reserved terms:

- SHALL NOT be publicly assigned.
- MAY appear in planning documentation.
- SHALL become official only after approval.
- MUST remain protected from conflicting usage.

---

# 16.13 Cross-Reference Requirements

Every newly approved term should include references to:

- Related Foundation Standards.
- Related SACOS documentation.
- Parent concepts.
- Child concepts.
- Synonyms (if applicable).
- Deprecated equivalents (if any).

Cross-referencing improves discoverability and long-term maintainability.

---

# 16.14 Governance Rules

Future terminology governance shall ensure:

- One canonical definition per concept.
- Consistent naming.
- Controlled approval.
- Version tracking.
- Documentation updates.
- Platform consistency.
- AI compatibility.

---

# 16.15 Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Creating duplicate terminology.
- Introducing unofficial abbreviations.
- Using undefined terms.
- Replacing approved terminology without authorization.
- Publishing terminology before approval.
- Creating overlapping definitions.

# Summary

Future Terms provide a structured governance framework for expanding the Simplicion Academy vocabulary while preserving consistency across education, technology, operations, and platform development.

By standardizing the proposal, review, approval, publication, and maintenance of terminology, the Academy ensures that future growth does not compromise clarity, interoperability, or long-term maintainability.

All new terminology introduced into the Simplicion Academy ecosystem MUST follow the governance process established in this chapter.
