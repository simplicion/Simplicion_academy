# Chapter 3 — Lifecycle Management

> **Normative**
>
> Every Academy asset—including educational content, software components, repositories, datasets, AI knowledge, policies, digital media, documentation, and operational resources—SHALL comply with the Lifecycle Management standards defined in this chapter.

---

# 3.1 Purpose

Lifecycle Management ensures that every Academy asset is created, governed, maintained, and retired in a controlled and consistent manner.

A standardized lifecycle enables:

- quality assurance
- accountability
- traceability
- maintainability
- scalability
- organizational continuity

Every Academy asset SHALL have a documented lifecycle.

---

# 3.2 Objectives

This chapter establishes standards for:

- Asset Lifecycle
- Lifecycle States
- Ownership
- Reviews
- Versioning
- Publication
- Maintenance
- Retirement
- Archival
- Restoration
- Monitoring
- Continuous Improvement

---

# 3.3 Lifecycle Philosophy

The Academy adopts the following philosophy.

> **Every asset has a beginning, an evolution, and an end. Governance applies throughout every stage.**

Lifecycle management SHALL ensure that:

- assets remain current
- obsolete resources are retired
- quality is preserved
- historical information remains recoverable

---

# 3.4 Canonical Lifecycle Architecture

Every Academy asset SHALL follow the canonical lifecycle.

```text
Idea
  │
  ▼
Planning
  │
  ▼
Creation
  │
  ▼
Review
  │
  ▼
Approval
  │
  ▼
Publication
  │
  ▼
Active Maintenance
  │
  ▼
Revision
  │
  ▼
Deprecation
  │
  ▼
Retirement
  │
  ▼
Archive
  │
  ▼
Restoration (if required)
```

Lifecycle stages SHALL remain traceable.

---

# 3.5 Lifecycle Scope

Lifecycle management applies to:

- Learning Paths
- Tracks
- Modules
- Learning Units
- Assessments
- Content
- Design Assets
- Software Components
- APIs
- AI Knowledge
- Repositories
- Policies
- Documentation
- Media Assets
- Operational Procedures

No governed asset SHALL exist outside the lifecycle framework.

---

# 3.6 Lifecycle States

Each asset SHALL maintain one lifecycle state.

Canonical states include:

| State | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Proposed | Initial concept |
| Planned | Approved for development |
| Draft | Under creation |
| Review | Validation in progress |
| Approved | Ready for publication |
| Published | Available for use |
| Active | Operational |
| Under Revision | Being updated |
| Deprecated | Scheduled for retirement |
| Retired | No longer active |
| Archived | Historical preservation |
| Restored | Reactivated asset |

Only one lifecycle state SHALL be active at a time.

---

# 3.7 Asset Creation

Asset creation SHALL define:

- purpose
- owner
- scope
- dependencies
- metadata
- expected lifecycle
- review requirements

Every created asset SHALL receive a unique identifier.

---

# 3.8 Ownership Throughout the Lifecycle

Ownership SHALL exist throughout every lifecycle stage.

Ownership responsibilities include:

- creation
- maintenance
- reviews
- approvals
- updates
- retirement
- archival

Ownership changes SHALL be documented.

---

# 3.9 Review Lifecycle

Every governed asset SHALL undergo review before approval.

Required reviews MAY include:

- Editorial Review
- Technical Review
- Educational Review
- Accessibility Review
- Security Review
- Legal Review
- Governance Review

Review outcomes SHALL be recorded.

---

# 3.10 Approval Process

Approval SHALL verify:

- quality
- completeness
- compliance
- documentation
- metadata
- governance requirements

Approved assets SHALL be eligible for publication.

---

# 3.11 Publication

Publication SHALL:

- assign a published version
- update repository indexes
- generate audit records
- notify affected stakeholders (where applicable)
- activate lifecycle monitoring

Published assets SHALL become authoritative.

---

# 3.12 Version Management

Every lifecycle transition SHALL preserve version history.

Version records SHALL include:

- version identifier
- author
- approver
- publication date
- summary of changes
- replaced version

Historical versions SHALL remain recoverable.

---

# 3.13 Maintenance

Published assets SHALL be maintained.

Maintenance activities include:

- correcting defects
- improving quality
- updating references
- enhancing accessibility
- updating metadata
- improving documentation

Maintenance SHALL preserve asset integrity.

---

# 3.14 Revision Management

Revisions SHALL follow controlled workflows.

Revision activities MAY include:

- corrections
- enhancements
- restructuring
- modernization
- optimization

Every revision SHALL undergo appropriate review.

---

# 3.15 Deprecation

Assets MAY enter a deprecated state before retirement.

Deprecation SHALL define:

- reason
- replacement asset (if available)
- retirement timeline
- impact assessment
- stakeholder communication

Deprecated assets SHOULD discourage new usage while remaining available as required.

---

# 3.16 Retirement

Retirement SHALL remove an asset from active operational use.

Retired assets SHALL:

- remain identifiable
- preserve historical references
- prevent unintended reuse
- retain audit history

Retirement SHALL NOT delete historical governance records.

---

# 3.17 Archival

Archived assets SHALL support:

- historical preservation
- audit requirements
- legal retention
- organizational knowledge

Archived assets SHALL remain protected against unauthorized modification.

---

# 3.18 Restoration

Archived assets MAY be restored.

Restoration SHALL include:

- approval
- compatibility review
- metadata validation
- version assignment
- lifecycle state update

Restored assets SHALL remain traceable to their archived versions.

---

# 3.19 Lifecycle Dependencies

Lifecycle transitions SHALL consider dependencies.

Dependencies MAY include:

- prerequisite learning content
- software components
- APIs
- documentation
- assessments
- AI Knowledge Bases
- media assets

Dependent assets SHALL remain synchronized.

---

# 3.20 Lifecycle Monitoring

Lifecycle monitoring SHALL evaluate:

- review schedules
- outdated assets
- pending revisions
- deprecated resources
- archive status
- ownership validity

Monitoring SHALL occur throughout the lifecycle.

---

# 3.21 Lifecycle Metrics

Representative lifecycle KPIs include:

| KPI | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Asset Freshness | Current relevance |
| Review Timeliness | Review completion |
| Version Accuracy | Correct version tracking |
| Active Asset Ratio | Operational assets |
| Archive Accuracy | Archive integrity |
| Retirement Compliance | Proper retirement |
| Ownership Coverage | Assigned ownership |
| Lifecycle Compliance | Standards adherence |

Metrics SHALL support continuous improvement.

---

# 3.22 Audit Trail

Every lifecycle transition SHALL generate an audit record.

Audit entries SHALL include:

- asset identifier
- lifecycle event
- timestamp
- actor
- approval status
- affected version
- comments

Audit records SHALL remain immutable.

---

# 3.23 Validation Checklist

Before an asset changes lifecycle state, the following SHALL be verified:

✓ Ownership confirmed

✓ Metadata complete

✓ Reviews completed

✓ Dependencies validated

✓ Version assigned

✓ Audit record generated

✓ Governance approval completed

✓ Repository updated

---

# 3.24 Governance

Lifecycle governance SHALL ensure:

- consistent lifecycle transitions
- documented approvals
- controlled versioning
- dependency management
- historical preservation
- organizational accountability

Lifecycle governance SHALL integrate with all SAFS standards.

---

# 3.25 Normative Requirements

The following requirements are mandatory.

1. Every Academy asset SHALL follow the canonical lifecycle.
2. Every asset SHALL maintain exactly one lifecycle state at any given time.
3. Every lifecycle transition SHALL be documented.
4. Every governed asset SHALL maintain complete version history.
5. Reviews SHALL precede approval and publication.
6. Deprecated assets SHALL identify replacement resources where applicable.
7. Archived assets SHALL preserve historical integrity.
8. Lifecycle transitions SHALL generate immutable audit records.
9. Lifecycle monitoring SHALL be performed periodically.
10. Lifecycle management SHALL remain technology, platform, and vendor independent.
